S001	BANGKOK — A decrease in rubber imports by China and other countries is being partly blamed for a crisis in Southeast Asia's rubber industry.
S002	Prices have been falling below production costs.
S003	For rubber farmers in Thailand, Malaysia and Indonesia - who supply two-thirds of the $36 billion annual market - this has been when their crop hit a dead bounce.
S004	“They are suffering from too-low prices below cost of production because of the underprice from rubber futures or rubber exchanges,” explains Yium Tavarolit, chief executive officer of the three-country International Rubber Consortium.
S005	Yium says that has forced some rubber farmers to put away their tapping knives.
S006	“They have switched to other agricultural activities.
S007	Some are cutting down all rubber trees,” he said.
S008	Supply and demand
S009	The consortium contends that speculators are skewing prices and that the ratio between supply and demand does not justify such low prices.
S010	But analysts contacted by VOA Monday say traders are taking a "wait and see" attitude because of a recent intervention in the market by Thailand’s government, under control of a military-led junta since a coup seven months ago.
S011	Thailand is funding - to the tune of nearly $2 billion - programs that include purchasing rubber directly from producers as part of an $11 billion agriculture stimulus project.
S012	A key industry broker in Tokyo, who did not want to be named because of his company’s business ties with the Thai rubber industry and government, says such market intervention by Thailand "has always failed.”
S013	What the Thai government actually needs to do, he tells VOA, is “cut down rubber trees and control the supply.
S014	Otherwise they will keep on producing rubber, and the government is buying rubber, but the global market cannot buy such expensive rubber."
S015	The global supply surplus for this year is estimated to total about 300,000 tons.
S016	But the CEO of the International Rubber Consortium says Thailand and other governments have no choice but to aid their rubber farmers.
S017	Helping farmers
S018	“Immediate assistance is necessary," he said.
S019	"For a longer term there should be other sustainable measures to assist farmers to be more sustainable.
S020	But for the current situation there is no choice for the Thai government to do whatever measures to help rubber farmers who are suffering.”
S021	In Japan, a five-year low for rubber was seen in early October.
S022	The Thai government's support helped temporarily to boost prices, but they have sagged again this month.
S023	China announced Friday it would double the volume of imports of agricultural products from Thailand next year, including rice and rubber.
S024	The Oxford Business Group, in a economic update released Saturday, predicted reduced output and a likely increase in Thai domestic demand will help reduce stockpiles next year when some projections suggest the global appetite for rubber will increase, though still leaving a modest surplus.
