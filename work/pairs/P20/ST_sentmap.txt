S022	Illegal Foreign Catches Still Enter US Seafood Market
S023	A new study, published in the journal Marine Policy, on illegal, unreported and unregulated (IIU) fishing looks at seafood entering the United States.
S024	It finds 40 percent of the tuna from Thailand is illegal or unreported.
S025	That was also the case with 70 percent of salmon imports and 45 percent of pollock, both from China.
S026	The lead author of the report, Pramod Ganapathiraju, a researcher at the Fisheries Center at the University of British Columbia, said the U.S. and other major seafood importing markets, such as the European Union, China, Japan and South Korea, see most of the product coming in through containers and cargo ships.
S027	"The volumes are quite high.
S028	But, at the same time, the amount of manpower that is needed to make sure that illegal catches don't enter these markets is quite low in most of these importing countries," explained Ganapathiraju.
S029	"So they need to increase the manpower to improve monitoring, control and surveillance at their ports."
S030	A 2009 government report found that only about two percent of seafood imports in the United States were being inspected at the ports.
S031	There is no public data for most other major import markets.
S032	The U.S. Senate last month gave its approval to the 2009 U.N. Food and Agriculture Organization's Port State Measures agreement.
S033	It is designed to keep foreign vessels suspected of illegal fishing out of the ports of nations which have signed the pact.
S034	Ten coastal countries and the EU have already ratified it but similar approval from 14 others is still needed for the treaty to take effect.

S040	Campaign Against Illegal Catches Finds Industry Allies
S041	Long explains there are problems all along the seafood supply chain.
S042	"I was surprised to find just how easy it is to manipulate the system," he said.
S043	"The U.S., the European Union, Japan, all big markets can start to change the shape of fishing by demanding clearer understanding of the chain of supply -- that supply from the moment the fish is caught to the moment it arrives on your plate."
S044	Long, a retired British Royal Navy commander, noted that some seafood suppliers are becoming conscientious.
S045	"Different suppliers are now getting very interested in the supply chain and providing that evidence to make their product a prime product that isn't going to be linked to bonded labor or tax evasion or over-fishing or any other illegal entity that tends to be linked to these illegal fishermen," said Long.
S046	"We're working with suppliers in order to make sure that we can build a system that they can use and present to their customers in a way the customers will understand."
S047	American Richard Conniff, the author of Species Seekers: Heroes, Fools, and the Mad Pursuit of Life on Earth and other books related to animals, said more pressure needs to be applied on the distributors.
S048	"The distributors are the bottleneck," said Conniff.

S050	And they are the ones who can stop it by insisting that all merchandise be traceable and that it all be legal."
S051	Ganapathiraju at UBC said that during his research, they received a surprising amount of cooperation from distributors because they are not able obtain the same amount of fish year to year.
S052	"The catches are being depleted.

S056	Chinese Regulators Expected to Determine Global Approach
S057	Activists, such as the EJF's Steve Trent, predict China -- with a huge and growing appetite for seafood -- will eventually be the most critical player when it comes to combating IUU fishing.
S058	"The way the Chinese government and authorities act towards fisheries on a global platform in the future will determine much of everyone's policy," said Trent.
S059	Conniff said China and other nations have little choice but to make changes or face dire ecological consequences.
S060	"There's an incentive for China and for all these other countries to change and to change fast because at this point 85 percent of all fisheries are at or beyond their biological limits," he said.
S061	Additional regulatory changes are being implemented to try to stem the tide.
S062	Fishing vessels larger than 100 tons will have to acquire a unique identification number that will remain the same even if the boat changes names or flags.
S063	There are also plans for a global satellite vessel monitoring system.
S064	It would be able to track vessels which switch off existing beacons identifying themselves and their positions when fishing in illegal waters or handing off their illicit catches.
