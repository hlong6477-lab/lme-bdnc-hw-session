S001	How the falling Aussie dollar could hit your hip pocket even if you're not going overseas
S002	Beyond making overseas travel more expensive, a weaker Aussie dollar can impact any part of the Australian economy dependent on imports — from electronics, clothes and petrol to groceries.
S003	The Australian dollar hasn't had the best time of late.
S004	Its value against the United States dollar has dropped by around 10 per cent over the last few months, and it's currently buying around 62 US cents.
S005	Beyond making overseas travel more expensive, a weaker Aussie dollar can impact any part of the Australian economy dependent on imports — from electronics, clothes and petrol to groceries.
S006	On the flipside, Australian exporters generally enjoy a weaker dollar as this can make prices more competitive to international buyers.
S007	A number of elements have contributed to the weakening of the Australian dollar.
S008	Why has the Australian dollar dropped so much?
S009	Consumer expert and director of executive education at Griffith University Graeme Hughes said several factors influence the dollar's value against other currencies.
S010	"The market now expects fewer interest rate cuts in the US, making US assets more attractive to investors.
S011	This has led to an appreciation of the US dollar against other currencies, including the Australian dollar," he said.
S012	"Secondly, concerns are growing that China's economic stimulus efforts may not be effective, weakening the Chinese yuan and consequently impacting the Australian dollar.
S013	"And declining prices for key commodities like iron ore and coal, coupled with rising geopolitical risks and the potential for trade wars, have also weighed on the Australian dollar."
S014	Chief economist at the Australia Institute Greg Jericho agreed.
S015	"The prospect of Donald Trump raising tariffs on Chinese imports to the US means that for those products to maintain the same price, the value of the Chinese currency needs to fall.
S016	That in turn will affect the value of the Australian dollar," he explained.
S017	"The Australian dollar is often viewed as a bit of a proxy for the Chinese economy due to our exports."
S018	How does a weak dollar affect the cost of living?
S019	A weak Australian dollar makes imports more expensive, which can make things tough for household budgets.
S020	Jericho said this most significantly affects petrol prices, which are entirely dependent on global markets.
S021	However, other items can also increase in cost, he said.
S022	"If the value of the dollar falls from US$0.65toUS$0.60, that would mean, for example, that the cost of buying a book that costs US$20\\ would\\ rise\\ from\\ A$$30.80 to A$33.33," he said.
S023	"So a falling dollar will hurt the cost of living of households that consume a high level of imports, but it won't have an impact on things like fees and charges, rent and childcare."
S024	Is now a good time to exchange currency?
S025	If you have an upcoming overseas trip, a falling dollar could impact how far your money will go.
S026	"It might be worth considering exchanging some currency now to lock in a better rate," Hughes said.
S027	"However, if you're not travelling soon, it might be wise to wait and see how the dollar performs."
S028	What is the prediction for the Australian dollar?
S029	Hughes said predicting currency movements is "tricky business" but there are indicators to look out for.
S030	“The Reserve Bank of Australia's interest rate decisions, the performance of the global economy, and the strength of the US dollar will all play a role," he said.
S031	"While the dollar has weakened, the situation remains relatively stable, but there's a risk of further decline."
